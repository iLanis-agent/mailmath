Boundary: arithmetic only Mail Math counts mail with division and rounding: the sheets an envelope takes under a weight limit, the weight of a batch, the postage of a batch, and the trays it fills. It applies the counting as written, so it has not seen your mailing and does not know your carrier's rules, rates, size limits or surcharges, and it gives no verdict that a mailing qualifies for a rate or will be accepted. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: sheets of 4.5 g, a 6 g envelope, a 28 g limit, 120 pieces of 28.5 g at $0.73 each, in trays of 50.

The sheets truth

Under 28 g with a 6 g envelope, 4 sheets of 4.5 g fit, leaving 4 g.

The batch truth

The 120 pieces at 28.5 g weigh 3420 g, or 3.42 kg.

The postage and tray truths

The 120 pieces at $0.73 cost $87.60 in postage, and in trays of 50 they take 3 trays, leaving 30 spaces in the last one.
